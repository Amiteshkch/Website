Research Overview

Three Pillars of Spray Drying Science

This research programme integrates experimental diagnostics, numerical simulation, and engineering design to understand and control the spray drying process — from the breakup of polymer solutions at the nozzle to the final dried particle.

01
Rotary Atomization of Non-Newtonian Liquids

Characterization of spray formation in viscoelastic Xanthan gum solutions across dilute-to-concentrated regimes. Effect of concentration (0.1–4.0 wt.%) and temperature (298–353 K) on ligament formation, droplet size distributions, and Sauter mean diameter (SMD).

Shadowgraphy PDIA OpenCV Cluster Analysis Rheometry
02
Pendant Droplet Evaporation — Experiment & Simulation

Experimental investigation of single droplet evaporation on a Ni-wire, subjected to controlled hot airflow (298–473 K, 1–50% RH). Supported by a two-stage 1-D transient numerical model with Stefan-flow kinetics and conjugate heat and mass transfer.

Backlit Imaging MATLAB Model Arduino Control Python Processing Sensitivity Analysis
03
Spray Dryer Prototype Design

Integration of atomization characteristics and evaporation timescales to engineer a compact, portable spray dryer tailored for agricultural food product processing. Design informed by experimental inputs from Pillars I and II.

Research Pillar 01

Rotary Atomization of
Polymer Solutions

Laser Diagnostics & Image Processing

Rotary atomization of water-based Xanthan gum solutions was studied using Shadowgraphy and Particle/Droplet Image Analysis (PDIA). A custom shock-proof facility was designed for high-speed subsonic atomization studies.

A novel image processing pipeline — using edge detection and cluster analysis via OpenCV and scikit-learn — was developed to detect and quantify irregularly-shaped ligaments and droplets, enabling quantitative measurement of atomization efficiency.

Solution rheology was fully characterized using steady and dynamic shear rheometry (MCR302) and extensional relaxation timescales via CaBER-DoS, revealing shear-thinning behaviour across the dilute-to-concentrated regime.

  • SMD and droplet size distributions validated against LaVision DaVis software
  • Ligament-to-droplet fraction quantified as function of concentration and temperature
  • High Ohnesorge number regime characterized — 2 international conference papers
  • Effect of solution temperature (298–353 K) on spray characteristics documented
  • Two journal manuscripts in preparation (Exp. Thermal & Fluid Sci.; JNNFM)

Research Pillar 02

Droplet Evaporation —
Experiment & Numerical Model

Experiment-Led Modelling Approach

A pendant droplet experimental setup was developed, exposing droplets on a Ni-wire to controlled hot airflow. A novel closed-loop Arduino-based control system maintained air temperature (±2 K) and relative humidity (±5%) independently across wide ranges.

Droplet diameter evolution was tracked from backlit images using a Python-based image processing pipeline. Evaporation timescales and the D²-law behaviour were extracted as functions of air temperature (298–473 K) and relative humidity (1–50%).

A two-stage 1-D transient numerical model — implemented in MATLAB — solves the coupled mass and energy conservation equations incorporating Stefan-flow kinetics, conjugate heat and mass transfer, and temperature/concentration-dependent material properties.

  • Evaporation timescales measured and modelled across full operating ranges
  • Sensitivity analysis on air temperature, humidity, and droplet size
  • Model validated against experimental pendant droplet data
  • Results directly fed into spray dryer residence time design
  • Journal manuscript in preparation (Int. J. Heat & Mass Transfer)

Research Pillar 03

Spray Dryer Prototype Design

Integrating atomization characterization and single-droplet evaporation data to engineer a compact, portable spray dryer for agricultural food products.

Design Philosophy

Droplet sizes obtained from Pillar I and evaporation timescales from Pillar II defined the key design inputs — chamber residence time, drying air temperature, and throughput requirements — ensuring the prototype is physically grounded in experimental data.

Target Application

Compact, portable spray dryer tailored for agricultural food product processing at laboratory or pilot scale. Designed for low-cost deployment relevant to food technology and agri-processing sectors.
